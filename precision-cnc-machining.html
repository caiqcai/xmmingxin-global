How We Hold ±0.01mm

At this tolerance level, the machine is rarely the limiting factor. These are the four variables we control.

Thermal Management

Aluminum expands roughly 23 microns per metre per degree Celsius. A 200mm part that warms by 5°C during cutting grows past its entire tolerance band. We measure at 20°C and let parts stabilise before inspection, so the number on the report is the number at your assembly line.

Light Finishing Passes

One heavy finishing cut leaves deflection marks and tool pressure in the surface. Critical features get semi-finish followed by multiple light finishing passes, which keeps cutting forces low and dimensional repeatability high across the whole run.

In-Process Gauging

Carbide tooling wears progressively. On a long run the last part can measure 10–15 microns smaller than the first unless the offset is corrected. We gauge in-process and adjust tool offsets rather than discovering the drift at final inspection.

CMM Verification

You cannot hold ±0.01mm with a caliper. Critical dimensions are verified on a ZEISS CMM, with a 2D projector for profile work, a roughness tester for surface finish, and a hardness tester for material condition. The inspection report carries actual measured values, not pass marks.

Frequently Asked Questions

What tolerance can your precision CNC machining achieve?

Our standard general tolerance is ±0.1mm on non-critical features. On functional features such as bearing bores, mating faces and seal surfaces we hold ±0.01mm, verified on a ZEISS CMM. Tolerances are confirmed in writing before the order is accepted, so there is no ambiguity about what we are committing to.

What is the minimum order quantity?

There is no fixed MOQ. We regularly machine single prototype parts and production runs in the thousands. Because CNC machining is programmed rather than tooled, the economical quantity depends on setup amortisation rather than a minimum threshold — we will tell you in the quote where the next price break sits.

Do you provide first article inspection reports?

Yes. Every new part receives a first article inspection report listing each drawing dimension with its measured value, the instrument used, and the inspector. We send the FAI for your approval before the production run continues, which is your cheapest opportunity to catch a misread drawing.

What file formats do you accept?

STEP and IGES are preferred for 3D models, with DWG or DXF for 2D drawings and PDF for reference. STL is accepted for 3D printing projects. If you only have a hand sketch, send it — we can model it and confirm the geometry with you before quoting.

How long does a quote take?

Quotes are returned within 24 hours for standard parts. Complex assemblies or parts requiring special tooling may take up to 48 hours, and we will tell you immediately if that is the case rather than letting the request sit unanswered.

Do you sign NDAs?

Yes, and we prefer to sign one before drawings are shared. Our standard NDA covers drawing confidentiality, a prohibition on subcontracting without your consent, and a commitment not to display your parts in our portfolio. If you have your own template, send it and we will review it.

Can you machine both prototypes and production quantities?

Yes. Prototype and low-volume work runs on the same machines and inspection equipment as production. That matters because the process does not change between the sample you approve and the parts you receive — there is no separate “sample workshop” with different capability.

What materials do you machine?

Aluminum alloys including 6061-T6 and 7075-T6, stainless steels including 304, 316L and 17-4PH, titanium Grade 5, brass and copper alloys, and engineering plastics such as POM, PEEK, ABS and PC. Material certificates are available on request for any grade.
